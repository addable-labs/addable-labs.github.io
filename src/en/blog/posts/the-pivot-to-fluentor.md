In early November nivå — Swedish for "level" — got a new name. It is now called
[Fluentor](https://fluentor.se/en/), with a new logo and a new landing page to
match. It is still the self-assessment of AI fluency that we described in
[Lessons from building nivå](/blog/lessons-from-building-niva/). But since
September we had been building what comes after the assessment, while the name,
the logo and the page still talked about where the journey starts.

## Name, logo and headline were about your level

The name told you what you would get: a level. In each area you read five
statements about how you work and pick the one that fits you best. That puts you
somewhere on the scale from *Explorer*, who asks AI now and then, to *Agentic*,
who sets the limits and lets AI agents do the work.

The logo showed it too: five bars of rising height in colours from deep red to
yellow, the kind of bars that show a level. And the landing page opened with the
line "Five statements. One of them is you."

All three said the same thing: this is where you stand. That is good to know,
but a level only tells you where you stood on the day you answered. It says
nothing about what to do next. A first-time visitor could easily have taken nivå
for a test, and a test is over once you have your answer. What we had built was
a route, but that was not what you saw first.

## What comes after the assessment

The assessment takes about five minutes. Reaching the next level can take weeks,
and that journey is what the product is about now. Getting there takes three
steps: see where you stand, take the next step, keep going. The assessment is
the first step, not the whole product.

Your result shows your level in each area and what the next level asks of you.
In the example result on the page, a tap on the area Planning brings up "Let an
AI agent draft the plan for your next big task. Go through it before anyone
starts coding." From there you pick one next step and get tips for taking it.
When your work changes, you can update a single area instead of starting over.
And Fluentor keeps every result, so you can see which way you are heading.

The tips are at the centre of it. A library of advice is a shelf you have to
walk to, and nothing on a shelf knows where you stand. A step you pick with your
result in hand starts where you are, and the tips come with the step. We call
that part of the product Guidance, and it is there for individuals and teams
alike.

None of this came with the new name. When we wrote the new page and checked its
text against the product, the next step, the tips and the history of your
results were all there already.

## A name for the journey

Fluentor comes from AI Fluency Mentor: fluency and mentor in one word. The name
also had to work in two languages, because the product is in Swedish and
English. The old name was easy to understand in Swedish but means nothing in
English.

The headline went the same way, from level to journey. The founder read one
proposed headline as saying that getting better takes weeks. To him that sounded
like a burden, so he asked for the word journey instead, with Fluentor as the
one who supports you or mentors you along the way.

So the page now opens with "Your AI journey, with Fluentor as mentor." The old
page asked which of five statements you are. The new one starts with the way
forward. The weeks are still there in the section "Between assessments", but now
the page also says that Fluentor supports you all the way. The footer sums it up
in one line: "From your first assessment, towards AI fluency."

## A torch drawn as a map

The new logo is a burning torch, the torch of knowledge: knowledge that lights
the way and is passed on from hand to hand. Up close, the flame turns out to be
nodes and lines, and so does the handle, which narrows to a single node at the
foot. Every node is a step on the way to AI fluency, and every line a path
between two steps. We traced the torch from the founder's own reference image.

The old bars showed how far up you were. The map in the torch shows where you
can go from there. When you open the page, the torch in the header lights up,
foot first and flame last. We hope the animation further reinforces the
product's new message and conveys our vision.

## What we kept

Inside the product the five bars still show your level, now as a meter, and that
is where they belong. The logo has to show the whole product.

In the torch, the nodes share their colour scale with the levels, from a dark
red-brown at the foot to yellow at the flame. The name keeps its old typeface.
The founder asked for that when the n in the new logo had come out in a
different shape. And the word nivå has not gone away either: the Swedish page
still talks about five nivåer and the next nivå, and the English page has the
same five levels under the heading "Five levels along the way".

## What we believe

We really believe the change makes it clearer what Fluentor does. We hope it
leads to more users creating an account, developing their skills and setting out
on their AI journey. As soon as we launch and let users in, we will see whether
we got it right.
